MY ACTUAL ROI SNIPS RESEARCH ARCHITECTURE
Date: 2026-05-20

What this file is
This is the architecture I use, or should use, to find a short-term stock for Charles.
It is not a deep-research run and it is not a Grok evaluation. It is the operating model:
what I look at, how I rank it, what I reject, and how I turn research into one trade idea.

Mission
Find one best long-only U.S. stock or ETF idea for a same-day to 1-3 day speculative trade.

The target is not safety. The target is asymmetric upside:
- fresh hard catalyst
- strong attention acceleration
- volatile enough to move
- tradeable enough to enter and exit
- clear thesis-break level
- clear sell triggers

The default answer should not be a generic watchlist. It should be one decisive best pick,
with a few backups only when they help explain why the winner is better.

Non-negotiable constraints
- Long-only U.S. stocks and ETFs.
- No options.
- No shorting.
- No margin behavior.
- One open position max.
- Small speculative position assumption.
- Entry should be compatible with a normal retail broker such as Webull.
- Execution/risk gates must outrank LLM enthusiasm.

Architecture in one line
Hard-catalyst discovery -> evidence ledger -> social/attention overlay -> market/tape overlay
-> reject bad setups -> rank finalists -> choose one -> produce an entry/exit packet.

Layer 1: discovery
I start by finding stocks with a reason to move now, not stocks that are generally good businesses.

Primary discovery lanes:
- Company press releases and investor-relations pages.
- SEC filings, especially 8-K, S-1/S-3, 10-Q/10-K, offering, merger, guidance, and material-agreement filings.
- Earnings releases, guidance revisions, conference-call headlines, and investor decks.
- FDA, biotech, trial, approval, advisory-committee, and regulatory catalysts.
- Government contracts, awards, enforcement actions, policy changes, and defense/energy/infrastructure notices.
- M&A, strategic alternatives, partnerships, licensing deals, customer wins, product launches, and analyst actions.
- Premarket movers, unusual volume, gap lists, high relative-volume scans, and momentum leaderboards.
- Retail/social chatter from Reddit, StockTwits, X, trading blogs, Discord/forum-style sources when available.

The key rule: social hype can discover a candidate, but it does not prove the thesis by itself.

Layer 2: candidate universe
The first pass should produce a broad but finite candidate list:
- obvious gappers with news
- smaller names with fresh official catalysts
- names with unusual premarket volume
- names where social attention is accelerating
- names tied to an active sector theme
- names with squeeze-like float/short-interest dynamics, if data supports it

Large-cap names are penalized unless the catalyst/tape is exceptional. Charles asked for
explosive short-term potential, so "quality company" is not enough.

Layer 3: evidence ledger
For every serious ticker, I build an evidence ledger:
- what happened
- when it happened
- whether it is official, structured, or rumor/social
- how fresh it is
- whether price/volume already reacted
- what retail is saying
- what professional or news sources are saying
- what could make the move continue
- what would invalidate it quickly

Official evidence gets the highest weight. Social/retail evidence is treated as an amplifier.

Layer 4: catalyst scoring
I score the story, not just the ticker.

High-value catalysts:
- FDA approval, trial success, regulatory clearance, or major clinical update
- merger/acquisition, buyout rumor with credible support, strategic review, asset sale
- major contract award or customer win
- earnings surprise with guidance raise
- short-squeeze setup with real volume and a hard catalyst
- major partnership, licensing deal, financing that removes near-term bankruptcy risk
- sector-wide move where a small-cap sympathy name has not fully caught up

Low-value or dangerous catalysts:
- stale news already fully digested
- vague "AI/blockchain/crypto" pivot with no numbers
- paid promotion with no official confirmation
- social-only pump
- gap already extended with no second catalyst
- dilution/offering risk
- reverse-split or compliance noise without real business catalyst

Layer 5: attention and sentiment overlay
After catalyst quality, I check whether attention is building fast enough to matter.

Signals I want:
- rising ticker mentions
- high retail discussion velocity
- premarket/early-session volume
- news being repeated by multiple market sources
- traders focusing on the same breakout/level/catalyst
- sector peers moving in sympathy

Signals I distrust:
- old threads recycling stale news
- bagholder communities with no new catalyst
- one influencer driving all attention
- low-float mania after the easy move already happened
- comments that are bullish but disconnected from the actual catalyst

Layer 6: market/tape overlay
A good story is not enough. The setup must be tradeable.

I check:
- current price
- premarket or early-session percentage move
- relative volume
- dollar volume
- spread
- liquidity
- intraday high/low
- VWAP behavior
- opening range
- resistance and support
- gap-fill risk
- whether the move is already overcrowded

For Charles's style, I should prefer a volatile setup that is still early or confirming,
not a slow blue-chip drift and not a name already exhausted by a vertical gap.

Layer 7: rejection gates
I should reject or heavily downgrade names when:
- no hard catalyst exists
- only social hype supports the move
- the spread is too wide
- liquidity is too thin
- the stock is already too extended without a fresh second leg
- dilution/offering risk is obvious
- filings contradict the bullish story
- the ticker is not practical for Webull-style retail execution
- the thesis-break level is unclear
- I cannot explain why the market may not have fully priced it yet

This is where "boring blue-chip crap" should get filtered out unless the evidence is unusually strong.

Layer 8: final ranking
The final ranking is not "best company." It is "best short-term asymmetric trade setup."

My ranking weights should be approximately:
- catalyst strength and freshness
- reason the move can continue today or in 1-3 days
- volume/tape confirmation
- attention acceleration
- asymmetry versus downside
- clean invalidation level
- tradeability
- whether the move is early enough
- risk of immediate rug-pull, offering, halt, or fade

The winner should be the name with the best combination of catalyst, attention, tape, and risk control.

Layer 9: final answer packet
The final output should always include:
- single best stock to buy today
- ticker and company name
- exact reason it could move sharply in the next hours or days
- underlying catalyst and why it may not be fully priced
- evidence reviewed, including sentiment/discussion trend
- suggested limit buy price or buy range
- same-day upside target
- 1-3 day upside target
- downside/thesis-break level
- timeframes to monitor after entry
- profit-taking triggers
- danger signals
- why this name beats the backups

Manual browser-mode workflow
When I am doing this live in chat, my manual process should be:
1. Pull current market movers and fresh news.
2. Search official sources for the top movers: press release, SEC filing, FDA/government/contract page.
3. Check whether the catalyst is fresh, material, and specific.
4. Check premarket or early-session tape: price, gap, volume, spread, VWAP/opening range if available.
5. Search social/retail discussion for acceleration, not just bullishness.
6. Compare 5-15 real candidates, not one easy headline.
7. Kill weak/stale/overextended names.
8. Pick one winner and write the trade packet.

Automated Roi Snips code map
The repo currently reflects this architecture in these areas:
- `src/research/scouts/`: discovery lanes for SEC, IR, news, FDA, government, exchange, social, obscure sources.
- `src/research/universe.py`: turns discovery into candidate universe.
- `src/research/cluster.py`: groups evidence by ticker/story.
- `src/research/ranking.py`: ranks candidates by research priority.
- `src/research/market_overlay.py`: adds tradeability and market data context.
- `src/research/gates.py`: applies research/execution eligibility gates.
- `src/risk/rules.py`: enforces risk constraints.
- `config/workflow.yaml`: research schedule and workflow posture.
- `config/risk.yaml`: risk parameters.
- `configs/live.yaml` and `configs/paper.yaml`: environment-specific trading settings.

Current weakness in my architecture
The architecture is directionally right, but it needs sharper live execution inputs:
- better real-time spread/quote coverage
- better premarket and first-15-minute tape data
- better retail/social velocity scoring
- cleaner comparison against high-risk small-cap movers
- less bias toward safe large-cap names
- stronger explicit "why this is not already priced in" scoring
- clearer handling when official evidence is strong but tape is already exhausted

Where Grok would fit, if added later
Grok should be a social/X discovery and sentiment lane, not the final stock picker.

Good role:
- discover fast-moving X narratives
- summarize retail attention acceleration
- find obscure ticker chatter before normal search catches it
- feed candidates into the existing evidence/ranking system

Bad role:
- choose a stock by itself
- treat X hype as proof
- override official-source checks
- override spread, liquidity, and risk gates

Bottom line
My architecture should be aggressive but disciplined:
find fresh hard catalysts, measure whether attention and tape are confirming, reject weak or exhausted setups,
then give Charles one decisive high-upside stock with concrete entry, targets, thesis break, and sell rules.
